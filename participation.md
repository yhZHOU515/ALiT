# Potential Participant Involvement and Evaluation

To support the development and validation of **ALiT**, we plan to involve **public librarians, Library and Information Science (LIS) faculty, and LIS students** in future evaluation activities. Their professional knowledge and perspectives can help ensure that ALiT reflects realistic public library service situations and relevant professional competencies.

Potential evaluation methods may include:

* **Surveys and questionnaires** to collect feedback on the relevance, clarity, and completeness of the competency framework, scenario taxonomy, and evaluation criteria.
* **Interviews or focus groups** to better understand participants' perspectives on librarian competencies, realistic service situations, communication challenges, and ethical considerations.
* **Competency framework evaluation**, in which participants review the developed competency domains, tasks, and mappings and provide feedback for refinement.
* **Scenario and conversation evaluation**, in which LLMs generate library service scenarios and simulated librarian–patron conversations, and participants assess their realism, relevance, difficulty, and professional appropriateness.
* **Human–LLM scoring comparison**, in which both human evaluators and LLM-based evaluators assess simulated librarian responses using the same rubric. Agreement between human and LLM evaluations can then be examined to assess the reliability of automated feedback.

These activities can create an iterative Human-in-the-loop development process:

**Competency Framework → Scenario Generation → Simulated Conversation → Human/LLM Evaluation → Feedback and Framework Refinement**

The goal is to combine professional expertise with generative AI capabilities so that ALiT can provide realistic, reliable, and educationally meaningful training experiences for public librarians.
